Interactive Writing Coach

Learn to interpret teacher feedback and use it to revise an expository paragraph.

Learning objective: Given a sample expository paragraph and teacher feedback, identify what each comment asks the writer to improve and revise the paragraph to address at least 3 of 4 feedback points.

Estimated time: 55–60 minutes. Use the navigation buttons to move through the lesson. You can read the feedback, listen using your device’s built-in text-to-speech/read-aloud feature, and type or select answers. No personal information is collected.

1. Welcome, writer!

Teacher comments are clues that help you make your writing clearer. You do not need to fix everything at once. In this lesson, you will learn a simple process: Read → Explain → Revise → Check.

  • Read: Read the comment and the sentence it refers to.
  • Explain: Put the comment into your own words.
  • Revise: Make one focused change.
  • Check: Confirm that the change addresses the comment.

Plan: Learn the strategy (10 min), complete three short practice activities (20 min), revise a paragraph (15 min), and complete the final check (10 min).

2. Learn the strategy

Four common kinds of feedback

1. Add detail: Give a specific fact, example, or explanation.
2. Explain your evidence: Tell how a fact supports your main idea.
3. Organize ideas: Put sentences in a logical order or add a transition.
4. Check clarity: Make a confusing sentence more precise.

8. Wrap-up: Your revision toolkit

When you receive feedback, remember:

  1. Read the comment and the sentence it refers to.
  2. Explain the comment in your own words.
  3. Make a focused revision.
  4. Reread and check that the revision responds to the comment.

Key takeaway: Strong revision is not simply correcting errors. It means using feedback to make ideas clearer, better supported, and easier for a reader to follow.
